# MENA Market Opportunity & Project Value — v4.2.5

## Purpose and interpretation

This evidence pack separates (1) regional need indicators, (2) evidence that a biochar or activated-carbon application can technically work, and (3) demonstrated commercial demand. These are not interchangeable. The heatmap is an ordinal **evidence-fit** score from 0 to 3; it is not a market-size estimate, probability of sale, market share, or revenue forecast.

- 0 — no relevant evidence located in this source scan.
- 1 — weak or indirect evidence (generic biomass-carbon literature, sector need, or feedstock inventory without feedstock-specific MENA application validation).
- 2 — promising evidence (a relevant regional study or MENA-specific process/application case, with material caveats).
- 3 — direct application evidence (a feedstock/application combination experimentally tested in a relevant regional setting or specifically reviewed as an electrode precursor). This does not mean commercial qualification.

The two downloadable registers are `references/mena_market_evidence.csv` and `references/mena_feedstock_market_heatmap.csv`. Each market-evidence record includes source URL, evidence type and limitations. Each heatmap cell includes a rationale and source URL(s).

## Regional demand and need signals

1. **Biochar market estimate:** Grand View Research estimates Middle East & Africa biochar revenue at USD 30.4 million for 2025 and forecasts USD 88.3 million by 2033 (13.9% CAGR 2026–2033). This is a commercial market estimate for MEA, not MENA-only measured offtake. It is not independently audited by this platform and should be labelled a third-party estimate.
   - https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/biochar-market/mea
2. **Land restoration:** FAO's 2025 regional communication reports more than 46 million hectares of agricultural land among 70 million hectares affected by human-induced degradation in the Arab region. This is a need indicator, not a biochar addressable market.
   - https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/fao-study-reveals-alarming-agricultural-land-degradation-in-the-arab-region/
3. **Water infrastructure:** ESCWA's 2024 Arab-region report states GCC countries account for about 60% of global desalination capacity and 40% of global desalinated-water production, with more than 400 plants. This shows water-treatment infrastructure scale; it does not imply biochar is a substitute for membranes or other established treatment media.
   - https://www.unescwa.org/sites/default/files/pubs/pdf/2024-progress-report-implementation-integrated-water-resources-management-arab-region-english.pdf
4. **Wastewater reuse:** A World Bank document reports that in Saudi Arabia in 2020, 18% of treated wastewater was further reused. This is a reuse-gap indicator, not a biochar-specific procurement figure.
   - https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099002211272487299/pdf/IDU-4b5501f1-f71f-45c9-87fa-509e7f3f0b80.pdf
5. **Agricultural residues:** A UNIDO Egypt project document estimates 33.4 million tonnes of crop residues, including 6.9 Mt wheat straw, 6.8 Mt sugarcane residues, 4.5 Mt maize residues and 3.6 Mt rice straw. These are national estimates; recoverable supply must account for existing uses, seasonality, moisture, collection and transport.
   - https://downloads.unido.org/ot/37/91/37919557/UNIDO%20IGGE%20Prodoc_English%20%28170146%29_Amended_20241202_FINAL.pdf
6. **Global biochar end-use survey:** The IBI/USBI 2023 report shows crop agriculture as a selected end use by 70% of surveyed producers, horticulture 28%, and water filtration 11%. This is a global, producer-side, multi-select survey—not a MENA demand split.
   - https://biochar-international.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Global-Biochar-Market-Report-2023-%E2%80%93-Public.pdf
7. **Market caution:** The IBI 2025 report continues to identify insufficient physical-product demand as a barrier. The platform must not assume that every tonne produced has a buyer.
   - https://biochar-international.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2025-Market-Report-Executive-Summary.pdf

## MENA case studies and platform value

### Date pits — MENA techno-economic assessment

Al-Rumaihi et al. (2022) designed a 500 t/day date-pit biorefinery and characterized samples. Their model reports 50 wt% biochar yield and 62% elemental carbon for slow pyrolysis at 250 °C, versus 24 wt% yield and 77% carbon for fast pyrolysis at 400 °C. The reported ROI/payback values are simulation outputs under study assumptions, not actual operating-plant performance. The chapter estimates about 800,000 t/year date pits based on MENA date production and pit share; this is a theoretical resource estimate, not verified recoverable supply.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-323-95879-0.50135-1

**Platform value:** compare process severity, yield, char carbon, coproducts, energy duty and economics for a particular feedstock instead of transferring one headline yield or ROI to another feedstock or plant.

### UAE — process-route comparison for date-palm waste

Martis et al. (2020) used Aspen Plus to compare pyrolysis, gasification and fermentation for UAE date-palm biomass. The study reports positive modelled economics for pyrolysis and gasification and a negative expenditure for fermentation under its process boundary. This is a useful route-screening example, but it is not evidence of a realized commercial loss, nor does it show the authors lacked screening—the paper itself compares routes.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/en13225877

**Platform value:** make route comparisons and energy/economic trade-offs reproducible for alternative feedstocks and project assumptions before selecting a process route.

### UAE — soil application of date-palm biochar

A 2015 Abu Dhabi sandy-soil experiment reported up to 20% higher water retention, a modest CEC increase and reduced sodium adsorption ratio after date-palm-frond biochar amendment. The authors called for longer-term monitoring. A separate Dubai study tested locally produced date-palm-waste biochar in saline-irrigated quinoa plots over one year and reported improvements in selected soil and microbial indicators.

- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.egypro.2015.07.729
- https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2023.1158784

**Platform value:** identify soil application as a candidate pathway, then require local soil, crop, dose and salinity testing rather than treating all biochars as interchangeable soil amendments.

### Egypt — groundwater adsorption using palm-waste biochars

The Al Kharga study prepared biochars from palm coir, leaflets, empty fruit bunches and date stone. Palm-coir biochar—not date-stone biochar—showed the strongest iron affinity (reported loading capacity 7.71 mg/g); the iron-loaded material was subsequently used for phosphate uptake (6.4 mg/g). This is direct regional application evidence, but column breakthrough, regeneration, leaching, spent-media disposal and plant-scale economics are required before commercialization.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.5004/dwt.2019.24516

A separate 2025 Egyptian study evaluated date-palm-fibre-derived biochar and activated carbon for phosphorus removal in wastewater treatment:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dwt.2024.100925

**Platform value:** distinguish adsorption-grade product requirements from soil-grade biochar and connect the feedstock and process to the contaminant-specific testing plan.

### Saudi Arabia — date-palm fast-pyrolysis TEA

Al Yahya et al. (2021) model a 10 t/day fast-pyrolysis unit and report attractive financial indicators. The analysis uses assumed product prices, and the paper contains a discrepancy between the gross product income and net savings wording in its discussion/conclusion. Reproduce the underlying tables and boundaries before using its headline economics as a benchmark.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/en14196048

**Platform value:** sensitivity analysis and break-even calculations can show how strongly a result depends on char/by-product prices, feedstock acquisition and utilities. They do not replace buyer offtake quotes or pilot operating data.

## Application maturity and technical gates

- **Soil / horticulture:** the broadest established biochar end-use signal in global producer surveys and direct UAE research exists. Commercialization still requires soil compatibility, contaminant testing, agronomic trials, crop-specific economics and distribution/offtake.
- **Water / wastewater adsorption:** Egypt and UAE laboratory/column studies establish technical plausibility. Scale-up requires breakthrough curves, competitive media comparison, regeneration, leaching, disposal and plant pilot data.
- **Activated carbon / supercapacitors:** global reviews establish biomass-derived porous carbon as a research pathway. This is not proof of a MENA buyer market. Qualification requires activation, BET surface area and pore-size distribution, ash/impurity limits, conductivity, electrode fabrication and full-cell electrochemical tests (CV, GCD, EIS, cycle life).
- **Construction / materials:** emerging use reported globally; MENA offtake volume was not established in this scan. Standards, durability, mechanical performance, fire behaviour and leaching are required.
- **Carbon removal:** requires methodology-specific chain of custody, lifecycle emissions, carbon-content and stability testing, durable end use/storage, monitoring and verification. The platform's current carbon-removal proxy is not credit issuance.

## The platform's defensible added value

The platform should be positioned as a **pre-investment decision-support and evidence-traceability tool**. Its value levers are:

1. Feedstock qualification and supply-chain normalization (wet/dry basis, moisture, collection, baling, transport, storage and competing uses).
2. Process-route and operating-window screening (mass balance, energy duty, particle heating, product split and process constraints).
3. Product-pathway matching (soil, adsorbent, activated carbon, construction or carbon removal) with application-specific specification gates.
4. Economic sensitivity and break-even analysis (feedstock cost, yield, utility prices, char price, CAPEX, uptime and logistics).
5. Evidence traceability (source, geography, year, metric, evidence type and limitations visible to technical and investment reviewers).

Do not claim a specific avoided loss or monetary saving unless a retrospective project comparison quantifies the counterfactual. The UAE fermentation case is a published modelled negative route outcome; it is not proof that the company lost money because it lacked a screening platform.
